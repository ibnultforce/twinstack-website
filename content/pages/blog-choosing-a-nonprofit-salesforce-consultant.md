                <p class="imp-text-xl imp-text-slate-600 imp-font-medium imp-mb-8"><strong>Short answer:</strong> choose the consultant whose proposal you can check, not the one whose pitch sounds best. Ask for hours and rates line by line, real nonprofit references, a named person who will do the work, a clear data migration plan, and a support plan after go-live. A good nonprofit Salesforce consultant will also tell you what not to build, and when NPSP is fine and you do not need to move to Nonprofit Cloud yet.</p>
                <h2 id="why-choosing-well-matters-more-for-nonprofits">Why choosing well matters more for nonprofits</h2>
<p>Nonprofits usually have one shot at funding a Salesforce project, small internal teams, and data that includes donors and sometimes vulnerable people. A failed implementation does not just cost money. It damages trust in the system for years. The ten questions below help you compare proposals fairly.</p>
<h2 id="the-ten-questions">The ten questions</h2>
<figure class="imp-ts-fig"><a href="https://res.cloudinary.com/du42kbmmn/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto/v1792143943/10-body-choosing-a-nonprofit-salesforce-consultant_esht66.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener" aria-label="Open the full-size image"><img src="https://res.cloudinary.com/du42kbmmn/image/upload/w_1400,f_auto,q_auto/v1792143943/10-body-choosing-a-nonprofit-salesforce-consultant_esht66.jpg" alt="Ten questions to ask a nonprofit Salesforce consultant and five red flags to watch for" width="1200" height="675" loading="lazy"></a><figcaption>Ten questions to ask any partner, and five red flags. <span>Click to enlarge.</span></figcaption></figure>
<h3>1. Which nonprofits like ours have you worked with?</h3>
<p>Ask for organisations of similar size and type, and for permission to speak to one. A partner who has only implemented sales teams will learn NPSP or Nonprofit Cloud on your budget.</p>
<h3>2. NPSP or Nonprofit Cloud, and why?</h3>
<p>A good answer depends on your situation. Be wary of a partner who says you must migrate immediately without asking what problem it solves. Our <a href="/blog/npsp-vs-nonprofit-cloud.html">NPSP vs Nonprofit Cloud guide</a> and <a href="/blog/should-you-migrate-npsp-to-nonprofit-cloud.html">decision guide</a> show what a balanced answer looks like.</p>
<h3>3. How many hours is each line, at what rate?</h3>
<p>Every implementation quote is hours times a rate. If a quote cannot be broken down into hours, ask for the breakdown. Our <a href="/blog/salesforce-implementation-cost.html">implementation cost guide</a> shows the typical lines and what they usually cost.</p>
<h3>4. Who will actually do the work?</h3>
<p>Ask for names and certifications of the people on your project, not just the firm's credentials. Check that the person you meet in the sales process is involved in delivery.</p>
<h3>5. What is your data migration plan?</h3>
<p>Look for a stable ID on every record, a defined load order, several rehearsals and reconciliation of gift totals after each one. Our <a href="/blog/npsp-to-nonprofit-cloud-data-mapping.html">data mapping guide</a> shows what a good plan includes.</p>
<h3>6. Who cleans the data?</h3>
<p>Clean-up is often the hidden cost. Agree in writing what the partner will do and what your staff will do. See our <a href="/blog/nonprofit-donor-data-cleanup-salesforce.html">donor data clean-up guide</a>.</p>
<h3>7. What is in phase one, and what is deliberately out?</h3>
<p>A good proposal is small enough to finish. Donors and volunteers first, programmes second, portals third is a common and sensible order.</p>
<h3>8. Will you help with Power of Us?</h3>
<p>The application is straightforward, but a partner who helps with it and plans around the 10 free licences shows they understand nonprofit budgets. Our <a href="/blog/how-to-apply-salesforce-power-of-us.html">Power of Us application guide</a> covers the steps.</p>
<h3>9. What does support cost after go-live?</h3>
<p>Most of a system's life happens after launch. Ask what a monthly support block costs, what it covers, and how quickly urgent issues are answered.</p>
<h3>10. How do you handle access and data protection?</h3>
<p>Ask how permissions are designed, how access is reviewed when staff leave, and where your data is processed. Our illustrative <a href="/case-studies/meridian-family-services.html">Meridian Family Services case study</a> shows what an offboarding gap looks like.</p>
<h2 id="comparing-onshore-and-offshore-partners">Comparing onshore and offshore partners</h2>
<p>The same project can cost very different amounts depending on where the team is. In our model, one 25-user project costs about $53,500 in year-one services at $65 an hour and about $144,100 at $175 an hour. Neither is automatically right. Our guide to <a href="/blog/offshore-vs-onshore-salesforce-implementation.html">offshore vs onshore Salesforce implementation</a> explains when each makes sense.</p>
<h2 id="red-flags">Red flags</h2>
<ul>
<li>A fixed price with no hours behind it.</li>
<li>No nonprofit references.</li>
<li>"We will migrate everything as it is" with no clean-up plan.</li>
<li>No mention of support after go-live.</li>
<li>Pressure to migrate from NPSP immediately, without a reason tied to your goals.</li>
</ul>

                <h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 class="imp-q">How do I choose a nonprofit Salesforce consultant?</h3>
<p>Compare proposals on nonprofit experience, a line-by-line breakdown of hours and rates, the named people doing the work, the data migration plan and the support plan after go-live.</p>
<h3 class="imp-q">How much does a nonprofit Salesforce consultant cost?</h3>
<p>It depends on hours and rates. In our model, a 25-user project costs about $53,500 in year-one services at $65 an hour and about $144,100 at $175 an hour. Small nonprofit builds can cost much less.</p>
<h3 class="imp-q">Should our consultant recommend Nonprofit Cloud or NPSP?</h3>
<p>It depends on your situation. New implementations usually use Nonprofit Cloud, which is what Power of Us now donates. Existing NPSP orgs should move only when Nonprofit Cloud solves a real problem.</p>
<h3 class="imp-q">What questions should we ask a Salesforce partner?</h3>
<p>Ask about similar nonprofit clients, hours and rates per line, who does the work, the data migration and clean-up plan, phase one scope, Power of Us help, support costs and data protection.</p>
<h3 class="imp-q">Are offshore Salesforce consultants a good option for nonprofits?</h3>
<p>They can be, especially for well-scoped builds and ongoing support, because rates are lower. Check references, communication and time zone overlap as carefully as you would with any partner.</p>
