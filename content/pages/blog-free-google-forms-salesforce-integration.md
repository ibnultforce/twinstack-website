                <p class="imp-text-xl imp-text-slate-600 imp-font-medium imp-mb-8">
                    Most free integrations are trials or capped tiers. Here is how to tell them apart.
                </p>
                <div class="imp-border-l-4 imp-border-brand-500 imp-bg-brand-50 imp-p-5 imp-rounded-r-lg imp-mb-8">
                    <p class="imp-mb-0 imp-text-brand-900"><strong>Short answer:</strong> check for a task cap, a paid tier above the free one, and whether the connector itself is free or only the form builder. A genuinely free integration has no meter in its architecture, which usually means no third-party server sits in the data path.</p>
                </div>
                <p>
                    Search for a free way to connect Google Forms to Salesforce and you will get plenty of results. Most of them are not free in the way you mean.
                </p>
                <p>
                    This post is about telling them apart, and about why a genuinely free option can be legitimate rather than a trap.
                </p>
                <h2>What are the four kinds of "free"?</h2>
                <p>
                    <strong>Free trial.</strong> Fourteen or thirty days, then a card. The integration works beautifully right up until the month you have forgotten you built a business process on it.
                </p>
                <p>
                    <strong>Freemium with a task cap.</strong> A hundred tasks a month, or a hundred rows, or five hundred operations. Fine for a form nobody uses. The cap bites precisely when the form starts working, which is the worst possible moment for a lead capture process to go quiet.
                </p>
                <p>
                    <strong>Free on one side only.</strong> The form builder is free; the Salesforce connector is on the business plan. This is extremely common and easy to miss until you are three screens into setup.
                </p>
                <p>
                    <strong>Actually free.</strong> No card, no cap, no tier above it. Rarer, but it exists.
                </p>
                <p>
                    The reason the distinction matters more here than in most software categories is that an integration is infrastructure. Nobody notices infrastructure until it stops, and a capped integration stops silently. Submissions do not error loudly; they just do not arrive.
                </p>
                <h2>How do you check whether an integration is really free?</h2>
                <p>Before you build a process on any "free" integration, get straight answers to five questions:</p>
                <ol>
                    <li>What is the monthly operation limit, and what happens when I hit it? Does it queue, drop, or error?</li>
                    <li>Is there a paid tier above this one? If yes, what triggers the upgrade prompt?</li>
                    <li>How many records can I sync historically, not just going forward?</li>
                    <li>Who holds my Salesforce credentials, and where does my form data physically travel?</li>
                    <li>What happens to my data if I stop using the product?</li>
                </ol>
                <p>
                    If you cannot find answers on the pricing page in under two minutes, that is itself an answer.
                </p>

Nobody is harvesting your data, and there is no upsell hiding in the product.
                </p>
                <p>We think stating this outright is better than letting you wonder.</p>
                <h2>What does free mean specifically for Google Form Auto Sync?</h2>
                <ul>
                    <li><strong>No licence fee.</strong> Not now, not on a tier above.</li>
                    <li><strong>No per-response charge.</strong> A thousand submissions costs exactly what ten do.</li>
                    <li><strong>No cap.</strong> Nothing counts your submissions, because there is no meter in the architecture to count them with. The form posts directly to your own org. Google's standard Apps Script quotas still apply.</li>
                    <li><strong>No connected-forms limit.</strong> Connect one form or forty.</li>
                    <li><strong>No feature gating.</strong> The dashboard, the response viewer, the permission sets and the setup wizard are all in the free package, because there is only one package.</li>
                </ul>
                <p>
                    The reason we can say "no cap" without hedging is structural rather than generous. There is no TwinStack server in the data path. Your Google Form posts to your Salesforce org. We could not meter it if we wanted to. We put no limits on the number of forms or responses. Google's standard Apps Script quotas still apply.
                </p>
                <h2>What does "free" not include?</h2>
                <p>Being straight about this too.</p>
                <p>
                    <strong>Support is best-effort.</strong> We answer support email and we fix bugs, but there is no SLA attached to a free product. If you need a guaranteed response time, that is a consulting engagement, not an app.
                </p>
                <p>
                    <strong>It does one job.</strong> It captures Google Form submissions into Salesforce. It does not build forms, does not sync back out, and does not connect to anything else. If you need a general automation platform, buy one.
                </p>
                <p>
                    <strong>You still do the routing.</strong> Responses land on Form_Response__c. Turning them into Leads or Cases is a Flow you build. Allow extra time to build it.
                </p>
